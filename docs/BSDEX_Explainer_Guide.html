Cross-venue crypto stat-arb: what it does and why it doesn't pay

The tool watches the same coin (BTC, ETH) on a EUR exchange and a USDT exchange. When the gap between them is unusually wide and wider than all costs, it buys the cheap side and sells the rich side at the same time.

03The quantitative framework

1 · Spread

s = VWAPsell − VWAPbuy

The executable gap for the full size. Binance prices become EUR as bid ÷ EURUSDT ask and ask ÷ EURUSDT bid, the worse side each time.

2 · Z-score

z = (s − μ) / σ

How unusual today's gap is. μ = average gap, σ = its standard deviation, over the window. z = 2 means "two typical swings away from normal".

3 · Cost gate

Psell(1−fs) > Pbuy(1+fb) + FX

After both taker fees and the 1 bp FX cost, the sale must still beat the purchase. This is the gate that actually decides.

4 · P&L per trade

PnL = q(Psell − Pbuy) − fees − FX

q = capital ÷ price. Prices are the fill prices 300 ms (live) or one bar (backtest) after the signal.

5 · Carry (funding cost)

carry = capital × 4 × 2.50% × hours / 8760

Four pre-funded pools (EUR and coin on each venue) earn nothing while waiting. 2.50% = ECB deposit rate assumption.

6 · Sharpe

mean(daily r) / sd(daily r) × √365

Shown only with ≥ 30 trades and ≥ 20 days, idle days included, so a short lucky run can't print a big number.

04Why a self-adjusting threshold

The idea

A fixed bar fires too often when the gap is trending (it keeps looking "unusual" while it walks away) and too rarely in quiet, mean-reverting markets. The live bar rises when the spread is drifting and relaxes when it is calm.

What the code really does

T = clip(0.4 × 0.59 × (1+0.05·vol) × (1+0.1·|mom|), 0.3, 2.0)

0.4 × 0.59 = 0.24, below the 0.3 floor. So in calm markets T sits at 0.3σ: a low bar. The cost gate does the real filtering.

Momentum is a raw EUR amount, so the same setting reacts more on BTC (larger EUR moves) than on ETH. Worth normalising if the strategy is developed further.

05The five trade gates

G1Signal: |z| ≥ thresholdelse HOLD
G2Cost: spread beats both taker fees + 1 bp FXelse SIGNAL_GATED
G3Cooldown: ≥ 30 s since last trade on this marketelse SIGNAL_COOLDOWN
G4FX fresh: EUR/USDT update ≤ 5 s oldelse FX_STALE
G5Depth: both books can fill the full sizeelse SIGNAL_GATED

All five pass → SIGNAL → PENDING 300 ms → FILLED (booked even if it loses) or FILL_FAILED (book too thin or stale).

Check of the backtest's spread assumption (measured 12 Oct)

The backtest charges a 5 bps half-spread on Bitstamp. Measured over 5 minutes: 0.37 bps at the top of the BTC book and 1.99 bps for a full €100k order (ETH: 0.28 and 2.84). So the 5 bps assumption is deliberately cautious; it is not the reason the backtest loses.

08Backtest: before and after the integrity fixes

Bug 1 · look-ahead

The gate read the fill bar, so it only took trades it already knew would win. Result: 100% wins, zero drawdown.

Bug 2 · wrong FX

Live used the once-a-day ECB rate. On 2 Oct: ECB 1.1225 vs tradeable 1.1267, a fake 0.37% gap.

Bug 3 · instant fills

Paper fills had zero delay, so they could never lose. Now 300 ms delay, full-depth VWAP.

Before-fix figure from ENGINE_INTEGRITY_FIXES.md (F1–F3). The May 2026 reports in reports/ (e.g. 1,686 BTC trades in 3 days at 100% wins) came from the old engine and should not be quoted. 19 integrity and no-look-ahead tests pass (run 12 Oct 2026). Seven trades is a small sample: the sign is reliable, the exact € figure is not.

What this shows

The gap the signal sees on a candle is mostly gone one minute later: 0.22% → 0.008% and 0.115% → −0.097%. This happens even with no FX leg and even when bid/ask costs are switched off. The loss comes from the signal, not from the cost assumptions.

Kraken serves only the last ~12 hours of 1-minute candles, hence the short window. Carry for the window: €13.49. Break-even without FX: 0.16% (>$20M), 0.08% (>$250M).

The one-line answer

The market is already arbitraged to within a fraction of a basis point. A strategy that pays the spread and fees can't win; one that earns them might.
