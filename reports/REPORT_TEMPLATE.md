## 1. What This Is

This report covers a **cross-exchange statistical arbitrage engine** operating on
BTC/USDT and ETH/USDT between Binance and Kraken. The engine uses a rolling Z-score
of the bid/ask spread across multiple timeframes to identify and trade mean-reversion
opportunities. It is fee-tier aware: the same signal logic is evaluated under several
fee schedules to show how execution cost is the dominant driver of net profitability.

## 4. What This Proves

1. **The strategy logic is functional.** The Z-score signal consistently identifies
   spread divergences that revert within the configured window.

2. **Fee tier is the dominant profitability driver.** At retail rates (Binance 0.10% /
   Kraken 0.40% taker) the strategy is loss-making. At institutional rates the same
   signal produces positive net PnL. This is expected for a stat-arb strategy at
   small trade sizes — it is consistent with academic literature on microstructure arbitrage.

3. **The engine is fee-tier aware by design.** Switching from `retail` to
   `dekabank_proxy` requires one CLI argument change (`--fee-tier dekabank_proxy`);
   all other logic is identical.

## 5. What This Does Not Prove

**Be explicit — this section is what makes the rest credible.**

| Limitation | Why it matters |
|-----------|----------------|
| No true L2 orderbook data | Bid/ask is reconstructed from candle closes. Real depth is limited; real spread varies with volatility. |
| No slippage model | Every trade is assumed to fill at the top-of-book price for the full size. In reality, 0.001 BTC is tiny and likely fully filled, but this assumption should be stated. |
| No latency model | Cross-exchange arbitrage is latency-sensitive. Network round-trip between exchanges is not modelled. |
| Synthetic spread reconstruction | The constant bps spread used here is a simplification. For a production backtest, paid L2 data (Tardis.dev, Kaiko) would be needed. |
| No market impact | Repeated trading at the same venue affects the order book. Not modelled here. |
| Backtest ≠ live performance | Historical patterns may not repeat. Past performance is not indicative of future results. |

## 6. What Would Be Needed for a Real Deployment

For an institution such as DekaBank, the following steps would be required before
live trading:

1. **Regulated venue** — Binance/Kraken are not MiFID II-compliant. A regulated
   equivalent (e.g. Boerse Stuttgart Digital, Coinbase Institutional, or a prime
   broker) would be used.

2. **True L2 data** — Replace candle-derived bid/ask with real tick-level orderbook
   data (e.g. Tardis.dev full depth snapshots at 100 ms granularity).

3. **Latency infrastructure** — Co-location or low-latency VPS near exchange
   matching engines; co-connected network paths for cross-exchange legs.

4. **Compliance & risk review** — AML/KYC, position limits, pre-trade risk checks,
   MiFID II reporting obligations.

5. **Paper-trading on testnet** — Validate that order-routing code works end-to-end
   before committing real capital (see Phase 5 of BACKTESTING_PLAN.md).

6. **Institutional fee schedule** — Negotiate directly with the venue; do not rely
   on the `dekabank_proxy` preset (which is illustrative).
