1. Quantitative Finance Audit (Strategy & Math Logic)

The Rolling Z-Score Time-Discretization Fallacy

The core engine generates trade signals using a rolling Z-score over discrete 1-minute OHLCV data grains. The strategy calculates the spread basis $S_t$ and its standardized deviation as:

$$Z_t = \frac{S_t - \mu_t(w)}{\sigma_t(w)}$$

Where $\mu_t(w)$ is the rolling sample mean and $\sigma_t(w)$ is the rolling sample standard deviation over a historical window $w$.

  • The Structural Hole: Evaluating an arbitrage model on 1-minute historical bars introduces massive look-ahead and execution bias. A minute-candle close aggregates 60,000 milliseconds of tick activity. The backtester assumes that if a 1.5-sigma spread window is visible at the candle close, a €100,000 clip can instantly fill at that price on both legs. In high-frequency realities, statistical spreads are highly mean-reverting and flash open and closed inside sub-second intervals. 
  • The Production Fix: Transition your historical testing from discrete minute candles to raw Tick-By-Tick Order Book Snapshots. Your Z-score must be calculated in continuous event-driven space, where the variables update on every depth change (Delta Updates), rather than arbitrary time intervals.

Real-Time FX Synchronization Tracking Error

Your calculation layer builds a synthetic EUR price for the global leg by scaling the global USD/USDT price against a daily reference or delayed spot rate.

  • The Structural Hole: If your engine uses an static or delayed exchange rate proxy (like the daily ECB fixing or a basic REST spot stream) to normalize Binance BTC/USDT into EUR, your strategy incorporates an unhedged foreign exchange basis risk. Intraday currency fluctuations between the EUR and USD legs can effortlessly shift by 1 to 3 basis points ($1\text{ bp} = 0.01\%$) inside a few hours, completely eating up your slim arbitrage margins. 
  • The Production Fix: Implement a real-time, streaming FX liquidity bridge. The calculation layer must ingest live, streaming executable FX feeds (via FIX from bank liquidity pools or top-tier interbank venues) to dynamically value the synthetic currency leg on every single tick, completely removing tracking errors.

2. Infrastructure & HFT Engineering Audit (Protocol & Latency)

Serialization Overhead & Data Processing Cycles

Your current tool routes market data updates over public WebSocket and REST JSON channels.

  • The Bottleneck: JSON is a text-based, token-heavy string format. When your server receives a packet like {"type":"update","price":"67250.50","size":"1.45"}, the CPU must waste precious clock cycles allocating heap memory and parsing strings into floats before your strategy logic can even read the data. This serialization penalty can cost anywhere from 200 to 800 microseconds per message.
  • The Production Fix: Strip out all text-based protocols on your hot execution path. Transition your global integration layer to native FIX Protocol (Financial Information eXchange) or compressed Binary Feeds (SBE - Simple Binary Encoding). This allows your engine to read incoming market data directly into fixed-size hardware memory structs with zero string manipulation parsing overhead, dropping processing times down to single-digit microseconds.

3. Business & Financial Analyst Audit (Costs & Capital Drag)

Order Book Depth Decay (VWAP Slippage Overstatement)

The backtester evaluates returns using a fixed capital leg size (e.g., €100,000) against the top-of-book best bid and ask metrics displayed on your dashboard.

  • The Financial Overstatement: Regional regulated venues are structurally thin. A €100,000 market order will instantly clear out the top layer of depth and walk deep into the liquidity book, severely degrading your execution fills. By assuming your entire size fills at the top-of-book price, your backtested Gross P&L is heavily overstated.
  • The Production Fix: Replace top-of-book pricing with a mandatory Volume-Weighted Average Price (VWAP) Depth Sweep. The system must continuously calculate the true execution clearing price by slicing the incoming liquidity array down to the exact order depth required to absorb your clip size:

$$\text{VWAP} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} P_i \times V_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} V_i}$$
